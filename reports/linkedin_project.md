## Post Content:

How has the Netflix catalog evolved over time, and what does the data reveal about global streaming strategies?

To answer these questions, I built a complete, reproducible data analytics engineering project from the ground up:

🔍 **What I Built:**
1. **Data Pipeline**: Cleaned, standardized, and validated 6,234 catalog titles using **Python** & **Pandas**, decomposing mixed duration metrics and normalizing multi-value co-productions.
2. **Exploratory Data Analysis**: Computed empirical distributions across content formats, maturity ratings, and international production hubs.
3. **Relational MySQL Architecture**: Designed normalized schemas (`netflix_titles`, `title_countries`, `title_genres`) and authored 10 analytical SQL queries with performance indices.
4. **Data Visualization**: Produced 8 standardized **Matplotlib** charts adopting custom dark-mode aesthetics.
5. **Interactive Web App**: Built a full-stack **Flask** web application with 5 routes featuring real-time client-side search, query accordions, and responsive layouts.
6. **Testing**: Enforced reliability with a 21-test **Pytest** suite covering edge cases and route responses.

📊 **Key Empirical Insights:**
- 🎬 **Movies vs. TV**: Standalone feature films dominate the library (68.4% Movies vs 31.6% TV Shows).
- 🌍 **Global Expansion**: While the US represents 41% of productions, India (838 titles) and the UK (602 titles) are massive content powerhouses.
- 📺 **TV Longevity**: 67.1% of all television series conclude after only 1 season, highlighting high renewal competition.
- ⚡ **Ingestion Peak**: Netflix catalog additions surged exponentially after 2015, peaking in 2019 with 2,349 additions in a single year.
