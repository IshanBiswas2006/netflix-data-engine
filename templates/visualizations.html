Production Visualizations

8 standardized Matplotlib charts generated from actual catalog data, styled harmoniously with our dark palette.

Figure 01 Catalog Composition: Movies vs. TV Shows

Evaluates the proportional split between standalone feature-length films and episodic series. Out of 6,234 catalog entries, 4,265 (68.42%) are Movies, while 1,969 (31.58%) are TV Shows.

Key Insight: Feature films outnumber episodic TV shows by more than 2:1, offering rapid catalog diversity while episodic shows provide long-term engagement.

Figure 02 Content Production Trends by Release Year (1990–2020)

Examines the vintage of titles hosted on the platform. The majority of content is concentrated in recent production years (2016–2019), representing modern originals and contemporary studio licensing.

Key Insight: Content produced between 2017 and 2019 dominates catalog volume, reflecting the transition towards streaming-first production.

Figure 03 Top 10 Content Producing Countries

Exploded co-production metrics reveal geographic production contributions. The United States leads (2,610 titles), with India ranking second (838 titles) and the United Kingdom third (602 titles).

Key Insight: International territories (India, UK, France, Japan, South Korea) represent massive creative hubs expanding Netflix's non-English programming.

Figure 04 Top 10 Most Common Catalog Genres

Aggregates normalized genres. "International Movies" (1,927) and "Dramas" (1,623) lead overall volume, followed by Comedies (1,113) and International TV Shows (1,001).

Key Insight: Cross-border cinema and drama narrative structures form the foundational pillar of the Netflix viewing catalog.

Figure 05 Content Maturity Rating Distribution

Visualizes audience demographics across standardized maturity classifications. TV-MA (2,027 titles) and TV-14 (1,698 titles) represent nearly 60% of all programming.

Key Insight: The library is predominantly aimed at teen and adult audiences, supporting complex narrative themes over purely young children content.

Figure 06 Movie Runtime Distribution (Minutes)

Histogram of feature film runtimes with median (98 min) and mean (99.1 min) markers. Displays standard Gaussian-like distribution clustering between 86 and 115 minutes.

Key Insight: Standard feature films center closely around the 90-100 minute mark, with outliers extending from 3 minutes up to 312 minutes (*Bandersnatch*).

Figure 07 TV Show Season Count Breakdown

Shows that 67.1% of all television series on Netflix have only 1 season, while 15.4% achieve 2 seasons, and fewer than 10% exceed 3 seasons.

Key Insight: Steep season attrition highlights high experimentation with limited mini-series and competitive season renewal thresholds.

Figure 08 Ingestion Velocity: Titles Added per Year

Tracks the historical velocity of title additions onto Netflix. Modest additions prior to 2015 surged exponentially to 1,300 in 2017, 1,782 in 2018, and peaked at 2,349 in 2019.

Key Insight: Dramatic acceleration after 2015 marks Netflix's worldwide global expansion and aggressive original content budget ramp.
